MONARCH
BENEATH LAKE GRANBY

You're standing where a town used to be. Monarch was flooded in 1950 when Granby Dam filled this valley; record low water has laid the lakebed bare again.

This tool lays a pre‑flood USGS map over the satellite view, shows where you are, and lets you mark the foundations, road cuts, and timber you find.

One honest caveat, up front: old maps and phone GPS both drift. The app always shows two rings — how sure the GPS is, and how far the old map itself can wander. It will never pretend to more precision than it has.

Get the historic map onto the ground

Two ways in. The first is one tap. The second gives you full control and works with a map you prepared at home.

Load it automatically Recommended

Pull a georeferenced pre‑1950 USGS topo straight from the National Geologic Map Database. No screenshot, no tapping landmarks — it lands already pinned to the earth.

Use a screenshot Manual

Bring your own scan from USGS topoView and align it by tapping two shared landmarks. Use this for a sharper map, an offline‑prepared image, or if the automatic pull can't find one.

Finding a pre‑flood map…

Asking the USGS map service what it has for this valley before the water came.

Look at the overlay: does it show a town and a river, or a reservoir? If you see open water where the town should be, this edition is too late — switch to a screenshot of an earlier map.

Get the historic map

Open topoView already centred on the townsite. In the sidebar, drag the year filter to end before 1950, pick a quad, and use the viewer preview — not the raw “Browse” JPEG, which carries a paper border and can sit rotated, breaking alignment. Screenshot it north‑up, then come back and choose it.

Show where you're standing

The app needs your location to place the orange dot on the old map, so you can walk to a foundation instead of guessing. Your position stays on this device — it's never sent anywhere.

Next, your browser will ask permission. Choose Allow — “while using the app” is enough.

Point the way you're facing

The compass turns the dot into an arrow showing your heading — handy for lining up a road cut or wall running off into the distance. Optional; the dot works fine without it.

Your browser (on iPhone especially) will ask for motion & orientation access. Choose Allow.

Carry this valley offline

Cell coverage on the lakebed is thin. Download the satellite tiles and the historic overlay for the townsite now, while you have signal, and the map keeps working when you don't.

Caches roughly a 3 km square around the townsite at walking zooms. A few hundred tiles — best done on Wi‑Fi.

Mark a find

Foundation, road cut, timber, stone line — name what you're standing on. Each find is a survey point.

My finds

Press and hold anywhere on the map to add one. These outlive today's low water.
